# Workshop 2 — Risk Sources and Objectives

Relevance is provisional and describes why the pair deserves workshop attention; it is not likelihood.

| Pair ID | Risk source | Objective | Motivation/capability hypothesis | Relevance | Feared events |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RSO-01 | Cybercriminal | Steal customer data or extort the organization | Monetization; commodity credential and application attacks | High | FE-01, FE-04 |
| RSO-02 | Supply-chain attacker | Gain execution/access through an OSS package, model, image or build dependency | Compromise one upstream component to reach many users | High | FE-01, FE-02, FE-03, FE-04 |
| RSO-03 | Malicious or coerced insider | Manipulate data/model or export customer/commercial information | Legitimate access and business/technical knowledge | High | FE-01, FE-02, FE-03, FE-05, FE-07 |
| RSO-04 | Competitor/economic actor | Reproduce model behavior or obtain commercial strategy | Repeated legitimate/abusive queries or insider/supply-chain access | Medium | FE-05 |
| RSO-05 | External abuser with valid/compromised access | Bias recommendations, enumerate data/model behavior or exhaust service | Fraud, disruption or advantage | Medium | FE-03, FE-04, FE-05, FE-06 |
| RSO-06 | Accidental/internal process failure | Introduce bad labels, unauthorized version, drift or privacy leakage | Human error, automation defect, weak governance | High | FE-01, FE-02, FE-03, FE-06, FE-07 |

## Deferred sources

Nation-state and hacktivist sources are not prioritized without evidence of sector, geopolitical, public-profile, or mission relevance. They should be reconsidered if discovery changes context.

## Workshop questions

- Which pairs match observed incidents, threat intelligence and business context?
- What access and resources would each source realistically possess?
- Which pairs should be removed, combined, added or escalated?
